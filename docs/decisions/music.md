# Music decisions log

Liminal has one music engine: Lo-Fi. Sampled instruments, an optional private drum take library, synthesized bass and a held granular pad are rendered a phrase at a time off the audio thread and scheduled sample-accurately on five stem players plus the dialogue input (see [Lo-Fi audio](../architecture.md#lo-fi-audio)). Archive dialogue is chopped on the beat, and the film soundtrack sits under it as a background bed.

The film bed's signal path, which several entries below refer to: `ArchiveVoice` feeds an `ArchiveStrip`, which feeds `ArchiveMixBus`: beat-synced echo lanes, a dark room, a halo, a 28 Hz high-pass, a 5.4 kHz low-pass and a limiter. That output goes into `LofiMasterBus`. `ArchiveConductor` runs the cue clock on its own queue. The master bus has one finisher chain (target -14 LUFS), plus a fallback Apple chain (target -18 LUFS) that is chosen once at init when the finisher AU is unavailable.

Entries keep their numbers: code and other docs cite them (D1-D106 described a removed engine and were deleted, so the log starts at D107). Format: what / why / alternatives.

## D107. Film bed: keep the baseline effected chain and change only levels (2026-11-19)
- What: by the user's direction (commits ee8edb8 and f1a22d4) the Lo-Fi film bed keeps its baseline effected chain (sends, phaser, high-pass, two low-pass stages, tempo-linked delay). Only levels changed: the Lo-Fi context gain and the under-chop gain rose 2.5 dB each, and the bed steps down 18 dB under each theme return's opening bar. This deviates from the handoff's "lift direct presence and bandwidth" for the bed.
- Why: the user wants the chops forward and the film as an effected background, never a ducking key. A brighter or more direct bed competes with the chops in 1 to 4 kHz.
- Measured (final matrix, `docs/plans/mpc-chopping-measurements.md`): direct minus wet -9.9 to -10.6 dB; audibility index in gaps 0.13 to 0.21 against a C-B9 floor lowered from 0.20 to 0.10; bed 10.8 to 11.6 dB under the mix in gaps; low-pass roll-off 733 to 1008 Hz (reported, no longer gated). `same` has 0.03 of margin on the audibility floor.
- Alternatives: raise the direct send and open the low-pass (clearer bed, rejected by the direction). A brighter-bed A/B audition remains a possible follow-up and would be off by default.

## D108. Dialogue performance: motif mode by default, grammar as fallback (2026-11-19)
- What: `DialogueMotif.plan` is the default performance. The five grammar families (anchor, echo, callResponse, sparseReorder, textureFill) in `DialoguePerformer` run only when it returns nil (`DialogueEngine.prepare`, `DialogueEngine.swift:86`).
- Why: the user asked for a chop in every bar. The motif places a piece in every bar of each 16-bar loop and limits the full line to twice per loop. The grammar keeps its rests and rules for cues that cannot form a motif.
- Evidence: the 16-bar change (C-D1) measures 0.91 to 1.00 across ref, long, fast and same (bound 0.25). C-D4 (rests) is not applicable in motif mode and C-D9 checks the loop shape instead. C-D8 (accents 50 ms from a kick) is warn-level at 0.56 to 0.70, because motif pieces land on downbeats by design.
- Alternatives: grammar only (leaves empty bars); motif only (no fallback when speech is too thin).

  - Echo throws sit on most cell endings, with five repeats at -3, -6, -9, -13 and -17 dB over a 3.5-beat tail.

## D111. A fresh line every 16-bar loop, with a crate of recent films (2026-11-19)
- What:
  - `DialogueMotif.resolve` ranks lines for every cue, whether or not the pad bank exists.
  - When the loop's own film has no usable line, the loop digs a fresh, unblocked line from `DialoguePerformanceMemory.motifCrate`: the last four films heard, newest first, with their PCM, analysis and transcript.
  - A loop that could only carry the previous line takes a fresh line from a later cue in the same loop.
  - `scripts/analyze-lofi.py` fails any capture in which a loop's main bank is the previous loop's.
- Why: the user's direction was "I want there to always be fresh samples to use. That's the whole point." A capture played one line for all 32 bars. Its second loop opened on "Design for Dreaming", whose narration over music has zero clarity against the music floor, so it had no bank and the loop carried.
- Evidence:
  - `DialogueMotifTests.aLoopWhoseFilmHasNoLineDigsAFreshLineFromTheCrate` and `aCarriedLoopTakesAFreshLineFromTheNextSpokenCue` both fail without the fix.
  - The approval capture plays a UFO Interview line in bars 1 to 16 and an Ergot line (from the crate) in bars 17 to 32.
- Alternatives:
  - Trusting the transcript over the acoustic speech checks. Tried: the narration still scored zero clarity.
  - A non-word fallback that ranks musical, non-speech segments by spectral fit. The user's idea, deferred; it is described in the measurements document.

## D112. Chops tucked into the mix (2026-11-19)
- What:
  - **Chop chain:** high-pass 200 Hz, -4 dB at 300 Hz, -4 dB at 2.8 kHz (it was a +4.5 dB presence boost at 3.4 kHz), low-pass 4.2 kHz.
  - **Levels:** dry chop path 0.72 to 0.28 (-8.2 dB). Echo about 5 dB under dry (feedback 0.55), with a long, dark room about 5 dB under dry.
  - **Music duck under featured chops:** about -1 dB (it was -3 to -5).

- Why: after the calibrated clip the user said the chops were "way too loud... they need to fit into the mids and melody better. They just stick out frequency wise." - Evidence: in the approval capture the chops are within about 1 dB of the melodic stems in 250 to 500 Hz, and level with the drums in 2 to 4 kHz. The film bed is 10.2 dB under the mix in gaps (C-B9). The user approved it by ear.

## D115. Bass on the kick's body, landmark-keyed ducks, a released space duck, and master staging (2026-11-20)
- What: a structural bass accent aligns its own 40-160 Hz rise to the kick's (`LofiDrumLandmark.lowRiseFrame`). The bass duck is keyed by the take's confident kick landmarks (depth by the square root of relative strength), and the level detector is the fallback. The space duck takes its onsets from the scored lead notes, and the space carries a -1.5 dB trim. The chop chain gets a -2 dB, Q 2 dip at 650 Hz. In the Lo-Fi profile the master bus takes both branches in at -2 dB and the finisher input is +6 dB.
- Why: aligning the bass's 20 percent point to the kick's click put the bass bodies 10 to 11 ms ahead of the kick bodies. The adaptive detector skipped second kicks of doubles (C-B2 duck -0.48 dB at 72 BPM). The space duck held 3.1 to 3.6 dB of its 4 dB all the time. The pre-master peaked up to +0.9 dBFS.
- Evidence: lag median 1.3 to 3.9 ms (p90 2.6 to 10.2) on every case, from 10 to 11 ms; the bass duck reads 2.3 to 3.5 dB; space GR mean 0.8 to 1.8 dB with 4 dB on onsets; pre-master -2.9 to -4.4 dBFS; LUFS and true peak within 0.05 dB (`plans/mpc-chopping-measurements.md`, "Mix DSP finish").
- Not done: more lead level, a brighter lead presence, or a firmer lead compressor (user direction: melody instruments tuck in). Drum dips that pushed chop consonants forward contradicted the tuck pass.
- Alternatives: a lower detector threshold (still level-based, and it ducks on toms and bleed); a single fader after the input mixer (the pre-master tap would still read the hot sum).

## D123. Dialogue lines do not repeat until the films run out of unplayed lines (2026-11-21)

**Report.** The user heard the same quotes come back as chops: "We have all that amazing dialog in these old films we need to use it ... Repeats on chops should be super rare, only after a lot of usage. To the point where I probably wouldn't notice."

**Cause.** Each 16-bar loop leads with one line (D111). The rules across loops were loose and short-lived:

- A line could lead two loops (`lineUses = 2`), and only the last 8 lines were remembered (`lineHistory = 8`, about 7 minutes at 72 BPM).
- The choice was deterministic: the top-ranked unblocked line of the film won. The weaver keeps returning to the same 12 cached films, so once a film's best line left the 8-line history it came straight back.
- The memory was reset on every Stop and Play, so each session opened with the same favourite lines.

**Change.**

- `DialogueLineLedger` records every line that leads a loop, per film (`contentID`), as film seconds with a play count and the time of the latest play. It is kept in `Application Support/Liminal/DialogueLines.json`, loaded once, and saved on a utility queue at most once a second. Nothing caps or prunes it (A31: no content limits); a line costs about 60 bytes.
- `DialogueMotif.make` skips any line that overlaps a played line by more than half of the shorter one, in addition to the in-session rules (now `lineUses = 1`, `lineHistory = 512`).
- It picks among the three best unplayed lines (`linePool = 3`, seeded by the loop), so each film's lines are explored instead of always its top one.
- Order when a loop needs a line: an unplayed line of the cue's film, then an unplayed line of the crate's last four films (D111), then the cue's film's line played longest ago (`recycle`), and only then the previous loop's line carried. With footage pieces starting at random points of 12 films, the recycle step needs the reachable lines of every recent film to have been played.
- The coordinator writes the ledger only when a plan is committed (`DialogueCueCoordinator.commit`), so speculative plans that are rebuilt never mark lines as played. `resetMemory` (Stop) keeps the ledger. Under XCTest the coordinator gets an in-memory ledger, so tests neither read nor consume the user's played lines; since the D125 verification pass (2026-11-21) so do automated DEBUG launches (any flag in `DebugLaunchOptions.isAutomated`), which had been marking three or four lines played per three-minute verification run.

- `DialogueReorderGuard.protectedSpans` accepted the word after a clause end before checking the clause end, so "the other one. Sally," was protected as one span (number plus noun) and both "and then we went to the other one." and "Sally, this is mother." were rejected as protected cuts.

- `DialogueReorderGuard.protectedSpans`: a span never runs past clause punctuation, not even into the next word.

Entries ending in `*` match by prefix; the others match the word or a plain inflection, imperatives only the exact word ("minded" is not "mind", "runs" is not "run"). Line interest is `0.5 peak + 0.2 second best + 0.3 min(1, content share / 0.6)`, plus 0.08 for "!" or "?", 0.10 for a vocative start, 0.06 for an imperative start and 0.05 for two to seven words, times 0.8 for a connective start, 0.75 for a dangling function word at the end and 0.9 above ten words. No network and no model calls; the lexicon is taste and the thing to tune by ear.
- `DialogueMotif.rankLines`: value times `0.35 + 0.65 interest` (`interestFloor`), so a clear plain line still roughly ties a murky striking one. A line without text has `acousticInterest = 0.45`, so without a transcript the order is the acoustic one of before.
- The seeded pool holds the top three unplayed lines worth at least 0.85 of the best (`linePoolShare`), and the pick is a real `MusicRNG` draw on the seed and loop.
- Resolve order for a new loop (the D123 ledger still blocks every played line at every step): the own film's unplayed lines of interest at least 0.30 (`plainInterest`), then a transcribed crate line of interest at least 0.60 (`strikingInterest`), then any own line, then any crate line, then recycle, then carry. - `DialogueMotifFragment.weight` (the strongest word inside each cut) and `DialogueMotif.interest` are set in `make` and logged (`weight`, `interest` in the plan's `motif` element), for the hook work.
- `DialogueDirector.run` waits for the transcript before the first plan, up to `transcriptFirstWait = 1.2` s from the cue's start and never later than `firstPlanBudget = 0.45` s plus the commit margin before the window. Only the dialogue task waits; the bed and the music never do, and the transcript task is never cancelled on the timeout. In time, revision 0 is built with it (line chosen and cut by words from bar 1) and no refinement follows; late, the acoustic plan and the revision 1 refinement run as before.

## D125. The motif becomes a hook: one 2-bar cell on fixed steps, varied only at the phrase ends (2026-11-21)

## D126. Tight, gated dialogue chops: waveform onsets, per-hit gates and a step grid that follows only its own drum hit (2026-11-21)

**Report.** After the D125 hook build on Vision Pro: "it still feels ... like the chops aren't tight enough ... you need to pay more attention to the waveform of the sample so that, when you trigger the sample, it immediately hits the word on beat. And they need to be shorter ... it only plays when you hold it ... shorter chops that are always on beat but triggered, repeated, held ... as long as you're still telling the whole story." This entry is part 1 of that direction (tight onsets and gated pads, `docs/plans/mpc-chopping.md`); the 16-bar arc (intro, chop beat, build, breakdown, outro) is part 2.

**Cause** (measured on the D125 matrix captures `ref`, `same` and `transcribe` at 70 BPM, a sixteenth 214 ms, with a 2 ms RMS envelope of each chop in `dialogue-dry.wav` against its manifest accent). Pads started at the quietest frame within 60 ms of the cut (`Cutter.snap`) and put the first analyser nucleus on the step, so breath, room tone and the consonant played before the step (pad lead median 37 / 41 / 25 ms, p95 60 / 99 / 134 ms; 24 of 78, 113 of 161 and 54 of 78 hits audible more than 12 ms early), and a weak first syllable put the heard attack up to 176 ms after it (21, 72 and 37 hits with the half-peak attack more than 12 ms off). The most-played pads were the worst (three pads with 104 to 134 ms leads made 47 of `transcribe`'s 78 hits). Pieces played to their natural end (94 to 99 percent), 1.8 to 2.2 steps median. The scheduler was not the cause (72 of 74 even-step accents within 3 ms of a drum anchor), but the step grid was: `LofiTimingField.offset(atBeat:)` snapped a step without its own hit onto a neighbouring kick within a quarter beat, so step 8 landed 120 to 135 ms early on the swung pickup kick in 7 of its 24 `ref` hits.

**Change.**

- **Waveform onsets** (`DialogueOnset`, called from `Cutter.trimmed`). A 2 ms RMS envelope on a 0.5 ms hop of the 200 Hz to 4.2 kHz band (the chop chain's passband). The first syllable is the first local maximum (within 10 ms) that reaches half the loudest value of the first 300 ms; the sound starts where the envelope last stayed under max(2 x noise, 0.1 x that peak) for 15 ms; the beat frame is the peak's half-way crossing; a rise of 12 dB within 5 ms is a hard (plosive or glottal) onset. A piece starts at most 30 ms before its beat (`softLeadSeconds`; a long "s" keeps its last 30 ms) less 2 ms, at a zero crossing within 1 ms, with a 2 ms (hard) or 5 ms (soft) fade; the line keeps its consonant (3 ms before its sound) but at most 120 ms before its beat (an 8 ms fade when clipped). A transcribed piece never starts inside a word: when the start would fall more than 40 ms into one, its weak first syllable becomes the beat (`weakLead`, the first peak within 12 dB). Halves take their text from word timings ("is" | "completed." was a letter-share split), and a whole weak lead word trimmed off a piece leaves its text.
- **Accents read on the render.** The arranged pads (and the line) are rendered once each when the motif is made (`DialogueMotif.renders`), and the beat frame is read again on the render (kept when within -50 to +20 ms of the source one): the chain's 4:1 compressor from 13 dB under the cut's peak lifts a soft onset, so its rendered attack came 20 ms and more before the source one (a transcribed capture's signature pad read -23 ms on all 17 of its hits before this step).
- **Gates** (`DialogueMotifCell.Item.gate`, sixteenths). `DialogueMotif.gated` sets them on the D125 arrangement: a stutter repeat and the hit that opens one hold one step; a hit one or two steps before the next holds to it; otherwise the signature and the third are 1/8 stabs, the response a quarter, and the cell's last response plays out, as do the line, the reversed pickup, the landing, the return's quiet piece and the drop-out's split words. `DialogueMotifPerformer.gatedEnd` ends a hit 6 ms (`gateLeadSeconds`) before the step its gate reaches on the drummer's grid, or at a syllable valley or word end up to 35 ms before that (`valleySnapSeconds`), keeping at least 60 ms after the attack; coverage repair holds its hits 2 steps. Events carry `gateSteps`, `gateEnd` (`natural`, `gate`, `valley`, `choke`), `drySeconds` and `sourceFrames` (also in the capture manifest), and the hook log line marks gates (`8S/2`, `/c` choked).
- **Renderer.** A hit is a prefix of its pad's render (`DialogueChopRenderer.prefix`) with a raised-cosine release of at most 8 ms (a quarter of the gate when shorter) and its gain; a choke keeps its 12 ms release; a gated echo throw is the stab thrown into its repeats. Every gate of a pad therefore has the full pad's levelling gain. Pads without a render (the grammar, a repair piece outside the arrangement) render as before.
- **Step grid** (`LofiTimingField.sixteenthOffsets`, used for `DialogueTransportSnapshot.grooveOffsets`; `offset(atBeat:)` stays for bass and mids). Each confident kick or snare belongs to at most one step: the step it follows within 0.24 of a sixteenth, an even step it precedes by at most 0.24, or between them an odd step it follows (a swung "e" or "a"). A step without its own hit takes the take's median offset for its place in the beat. Offsets are clamped to ±0.06 beat on 8th positions and -0.04 to +0.19 beat on odd steps, and step times strictly increase.

## D128. Strummed hooks played off the drums in the guitar and harp phrases, with one echo throw (2026-11-22)

**Direction.** The user asked to "expand the number of instruments that are strummed, like more guitars", with patterns "a little bit more complicated, like not single notes, but rather sometimes a strummed chord", and for "lo-fi guitars or acoustic instruments" with "a very high bar". "There were a couple of times when it hits just right, but that was mostly due to echo." The model was Wu-Tang: "RZA and GZA ... bring out the like sort of solo or minimal strumming and work that into the beat and play it off of the drums." And: "Don't overdo it though ... We can't let this like take over and stand out or get too messy. You need to leave space in the mix, but it needs to be a little bit more."

**Found first** (source, at 5d21cdc):

- The guitar played one phrase in four, and 60 percent of the time that was the breakdown, where `LofiGuitarPhrasing`'s sparse rule allows no strum. Most takes had no strum at all.
- A strum was two or three triad tones glued under a melody note: no seventh or ninth, no velocity shape, no choke, nothing independent of the melody, and nothing in the guitar code read the drums.
- Every note rang for the program's release (0.28 to 0.32 s), so a choked stab was impossible. Every lead-stem note sent at least 44 percent to the echo, so "echo only on the last stab" was impossible too.
- The lead gain is normalized on the loudest 400 ms of the dry lead, so louder strums would have turned the melody down.
- The dialogue chops follow a fixed 16-bar arc (D126 part 2, D127). Guitar phrase 1 is take bars 5-8 (the chop beat and the build) and phrase 2 is take bars 9-12 (the breakdown, one or two held words a bar).
- The mix margins are thin (D118): C-B5 and C-B11 sat within 0.1 dB of their limits on some cases.

**Craft research.** RZA used guitar "like it was a sound he was going to sample" (Yoram Vazan); one strum as the main stab of a beat ("Incarcerated Scarfaces" takes the first strum of "Wang Dang Doodle"); exact repetition; and the documented failure case of too much played guitar (*8 Diagrams*, "trying to do too much of this guitar stuff"). So the hook is a few sampled-sounding gestures, never continuous comping, and it must not step on the chops, which act as the vocal.

**Design.**

- **Primitives.** `LofiScore.Part.strum`, `Note.damp` (a per-note choke in `LofiSampleBank.add` and `LofiSynth`), `LofiScore.strumEvents`, and `LofiPreset.Strum` (`.pick` for the guitars, `.roll` for the harps). `.strum` notes render into the lead's accent buffer: the lead's filters, gain curve and phrase gain, but never the gain measurement or the compressor reference. So the melody's level is bit-identical with the hook on or off.
- **Voicings** (`LofiStrumVoicing`). Root, third, fifth, seventh and ninth of the phrase's own chord, snapped into its scale; strings 3 to 9 semitones apart; the third always, the seventh when the chord has four classes; never a plain major triad; MIDI 45 to 72, the top string under the melody.
- **Strokes** (`LofiStrum`). Choked stabs (3-4 strings, 6-11 ms apart, a quarter beat, 25 ms damp), dyads, up-strokes, dead strokes, held chords (4-5 strings) and harp rolls (40-70 ms a string). Per-string level `L / sqrt(N)` with a taper, so an event's power does not grow with its string count.
- **Richer melody strums** (`LofiGuitarPhrasing`). Opening and landing notes strum up to four strings with sevenths. Everything else in D114 is unchanged and its tests pass with no bound loosened.
- **The hook line** (`LofiStrumHook`, scored last; nothing else in the phrase sees it). Five archetypes: A lone stab in bars 0 and 2; B call and answer an inversion lower; C a down-up "chk-a" with an optional dead-stroke pickup; D a held chord, then its top three strings as a stab; E a dyad hook. One sixteenth X is chosen for the statement against the drums (`LofiStrumDrums`: the take's kicks, full snares and ghost notes on the drummer's own swung grid) and repeated exactly. It is never on a snare, step 0 or 15, nor within 40 ms of a full snare, nor (low strings) within 30 ms of a kick, nor within a quarter beat of a melody or shell onset. There is nothing in a bar with three melody notes and nothing in the phrase's last bar. Even takes play A-C and odd takes D-E, so consecutive takes never repeat an archetype.
- **Turn-taking with the chops.** The score cannot see the chops, so it follows the arc. In the breakdown (take bars 9-12) the hook answers the snare (the 8th after it) or pushes into a kick. In take bars 5-8 (the chop beat) it plays only with a kick or on an even gap, at most twice.
- **Echo.** One event per phrase, the statement's last if it starts by beat 10.5, is thrown into the echo. Everything else sends almost nothing, and the choke opens the space duck again before the first echo tap. `LofiEffects`, the space chain, `LofiWetTone` and every existing row's echo values are unchanged.

**Round 2 mechanism** (in the code, off by default):

- `LofiBassVoice.Cue`: the note's own harmonics from `lowHz` up, in phase with the sub, the first at `levelDB` under the fundamental and each next `slopeDB` lower, weighted by the 160 Hz crossover's complement and a 4-pole roll-off at `highHz`, following the note's envelope and velocity. The layer is zero at the strike (`strikeOn`, else the note start) and rises over `onset` seconds (raised cosine), so the strike stays the brightest moment. The `synth` tone takes `synthOffsetDB` more. It renders into its own mono buffer. `LofiStemChains.applyBass(cue:)` adds it after the parallel tanh band and the compressor's makeup, before the EQ and trim, so it is neither saturated nor able to move the compressor, and with it the sub.
- `LofiCueDuck`: the finished bass is band-passed 180-450 Hz (the cue band, not the sub) and followed with a 10 ms attack and a 120 ms release. That detector keys a zero-phase 180-450 Hz reduction of the texture and of the return's output. The duck is full depth (3 dB) at the phrase's median detector level and linear below it, and 0 dB when the bass rests. The return is ducked before the bass throws join it, so the bass's own echoes keep their cue; the send and the feedback path are never touched. The texture is cut at the phrase edge, so its reduction fades over 10 ms at both ends. Chops, dialogue, dry (lead and strum) and drums are never ducked.
- Phrase render only (forward and backward filters over the whole phrase). Nothing new runs on a realtime path. To make the detector possible the renderer now finishes the bass before the space. With no hooks the five music stems and the film taps are bit-identical to 0a627bb (`distortion-report.py --against`, nofilm: G7 -240 dB).
- Hooks: `tune.bassCueDb` (`off` or dB), `tune.bassCueBand`, `tune.bassCueSlopeDb`, `tune.bassCueOnsetMs`, `tune.bassCueSynthDb`, `tune.cueDuck` (`texture+wet:180-450:3` or `off`). `LofiRenderTuning.defaultBassCue` and `defaultCueDuck` are the app's values, both `nil`.

**Round 3: the cue cancelled the body's own harmonics** (verification pass, 22 November 2026). Per note, R2a's cue made the round tone's MIDI 39 notes in `fast` *quieter* at h3/h4 by 1.5 to 2.5 dB, and the same happened to most MIDI 36 notes in `same`. The synthesized partials were in sine phase with the sub, but the sampled body carries those harmonics with the recording's own phase, and on these notes they were roughly opposed. That is why round/high got worse in every round-2 set, and why raising the level (R2c) cancelled more. The fix is `Cue.alignToTone` (hook `tune.bassCueAlign`, default `on`; `off` gives round 2's sine phase). The sub's own partial at k is added as a known in-phase term, and a quarter of the cue's amplitude biases the result toward sine phase. The partial then takes that one phase for the note. Its level is unchanged and it stays exactly harmonic. The synth's series is already in sine phase, so it is never aligned. Two rejected intermediate versions are worth recording. A phase that tracked the body over a sliding window flipped half a cycle wherever the body's harmonic about cancelled the bias, which clicked (sustain-only 1-1.25 kHz +10 dB in `fast`). With a guard against the flip, it still followed the upright's drifting partials, so the upright inharmonic share rose 2-4 dB (G6). A fixed phase per note has neither problem.

**Cause** (salience probe on the 12 cached films with audio, 95 windows of 30 s, 72 BPM, `scripts/dialogue-salience-probe.sh`; the diagnosis of 22 November 2026). With a transcript, 25.6 % of plays cut a word audibly by accident and 24.8 % were syllable halves by design; 17.2 % were function words only. The mechanisms: D126 gates of 0.2-0.4 s close inside content words that run 0.4-0.9 s (817 plays) and the next hit chokes the previous one mid-word (653); word-group boundaries snapped up to 60 ms into a word (695); the onset trim skipped a word's first consonant or syllable (124); every piece of 0.26 s or more was halved at a valley whatever it was ("tr-" | "-ip", "UF-" | "-O", "LS-" | "-D.", 755 halves of function words); groups joined only by length, so "It's a" and "Yes, they" were pieces.

**Change.**

- Word groups carry a content word (`DialogueMotifCutter.wordGroups`): a function word rides with the next content word, or the previous one when the run's largest pause (at least `groupPauseSeconds`) follows it or the line ends; at most `groupWordLimit` 4 words and `groupMaximumSeconds` 0.9 s (a connective group may join up to `mergedPieceSeconds` 1.1 s). A long run of function words gives up only its far end.
- A group boundary is cut only at a real dip (`isRealDip`: 9 dB under the quieter word's peak, or a 15 ms run 4 dB under it) searched `wordTimingSeconds` 25 ms into each word, else the groups stay together; a piece merged past 1.1 s takes its deepest boundary anyway, and a one-group line splits at its deepest word boundary. The acoustic branch keeps only real-dip valleys.
- Line units on word boundaries (`DialogueHierarchy.refine`): a transcribed utterance, phrase or word group whose edge lies more than 30 ms inside a word core is added again on whole words (`Builder.addAligned`, edges refined around the first and last word, pulled back a word when too long), and a `protectedCut` unit is added again covering the span it cut. The original is rejected only when the aligned unit can play.
- Splits are whole units. With a transcript only a word of weight at least 0.7 with two or more syllables by text and nuclei splits into 2-4 ordered `syllable` fragments ("psi-", "-lo-", "-cy-", "-bin"), and an initialism splits into `letter` fragments only when it has one nucleus per letter; never a function word, a one-syllable word or across words. Without a transcript a piece halves only at a real dip. `halves` holds 2-4 ordered units; `headHalf` takes the first syllable or letter.
- The onset trim never starts a piece more than 25 ms inside a word: the start is clamped to the word's start and the detected attack stays the accent, unless that leaves more than 90 ms before it, when the word's first syllable is the beat (`weakLead`).
- A gate that would close inside a word ends at the next word end (or real dip) within 0.30 s, else at the last one before it that keeps the fragment's first content word whole, else at the next one at all (`gateEnd` "word"). The D126 gate cuts only where no word is cut.
- A hit that would choke the previous piece inside a word ends that piece at its last word end before the hit, or is skipped (an unfixed hit moves on by 8ths, a fixed one drops). A marked stutter or follow-on repeat still chokes when the piece has no such end.
- Fit the cut to the room (`fitsRoom`, `cleanSeconds`): the motif stores its beat; chop-beat fills, the turn, the build, the intro tag and the outro tag take a cut whose first content word ends before the next hit, or drop the site; the turn's backbeat response is kept and drops what follows it. The groove's fill count per bar is sized to the section's dry budget (`grooveBudgetShare` 0.85 of `maximumSectionOccupancy`) so both passes of the cell play the same hits, and a fill goes between two skeleton hits of the same cut when one fits. The last response is gated to the next cell's first hit. Fills, the build's ladder, the breakdown's first word and the stand-in split pair use content pieces when the line has two or more. A fill left next to the same cut by a skipped hit is dropped (marked rolls exempt). Coverage repair uses whole content pieces only.
- An untexted unit's interest is `untextedInterest` 0.15 when the cue has a transcript (0.45 `acousticInterest` without one, unchanged). A transcript that gives no unit text changes nothing, D130's word rules included.
- The pad-bank fallback skips pads whose edges lie more than 30 ms inside a word core when the transcript gave text.
- Kept: the hook templates and two-loop rotation, the arc and `chooseArc`, the feels, the non-reorder rule, the dry budget and occupancy caps (the build now counts in the budget), the cue token and revision, the acoustic fallback and the threading. Nothing runs on an audio thread.
