The [engineering research](lofi-studio.md) records the primary DSP sources and bench methods. The handoff was written against `a0c3cddc5a7c`. This audit uses the new native Lo-Fi source and tools for current behavior.

### Controlled foreground compatibility check

A synthetic 48 kHz stereo vocal-like harmonic signal with broad formants near 520, 1,450 and 2,520 Hz was placed into two known foreground intervals. Each interval used the same dry and wet source amplitudes. In the first, the background signals were in phase. Combined background mean-square power was 0.01135 and the sum of independent powers was 0.00567, a 3.01 dB underestimation. The actual foreground-to-background ratio was −5.44 dB. In the second, wet and dry were oppositely signed. Their combined waveform had zero power while the sum of independent powers was 0.00566. This fixture exposes a phase-sensitive failure in a solo-power proxy, using known truth with no private audio or automatic speech claim. Use combined background samples for competition diagnostics and measure only where the foreground reference carries signal. STOI requires a clean aligned speech reference, suitable degradations and an independently versioned implementation. The local environment did not have one installed. [Taal et al. STOI paper](https://ceestaal.nl/Taal%282011%29.pdf)

An independent SciPy polyphase reconstruction check of the analytic 48 kHz/12 kHz phase-offset sine from the [research note](lofi-studio.md#measured-intersample-smoke-test) returned −3.0976 dBFS sample peak and −0.08718 dB reconstructed peak at 4×, 8× and 16× with a Kaiser beta-14 filter. FFmpeg's rounded result was −0.1 dBTP. This verifies agreement on the fixture. Broader fixture and target-device measurements remain separate.

### Close microphones versus the mixed break

I measured **22** readable, same-rate triplets consisting of the original stereo mix, mono kick mic and mono top snare mic at 44.1 kHz. Two normalized groups lacked a usable same-format/rate role pair in the inspected exports. One label stayed unmatched, avoiding an inferred pairing. Normalize on labels, then select the same-format, same-rate candidate triplet with the closest mixed frame count. Measurements operated on shared durations. Close microphones are source proxies with bleed and physical offsets.

1. Apply a zero-phase 35–112 Hz bandpass to kick mic and mix and form a 2 ms RMS envelope. Use maximum per-sample stereo power for the mix. Find local peaks with adaptive prominence and 105 ms minimum spacing. Fit a single per-break lag by envelope cross-correlation within ±100 ms in the first quarter. Score each remaining three quarters with one-to-one nearest event matches within ±44 ms.
2. Correlate low-band waveforms for up to 20 strong kick windows per break. Search a local ±16 ms interval around the fitted lag and retain the stronger channel case. The best absolute peak can land on the opposite half cycle. Mix EQ, summation and unknown gain prohibit a wiring or polarity verdict from that sign.
3. Use 900 Hz–5 kHz peaks in the top snare mic and exclude those within 80 ms of a detected kick-mic peak. Compare the local kick-band level near remaining snare peaks with median kick-mic peak level in the same break. This is a low-band co-activation proxy. Residual tail and missed kicks also contribute.

| Measurement | Observed result |
| --- | --- |
| Overlay duration difference | Median 0 samples and p90 1.9 samples. Maximum 64,487 samples or about 1.46 s. |
| Estimated mixed minus kick-mic envelope lag | Median +25.9 ms. Observed range −47.9 to +93.8 ms. |
| Out-of-training-window peak matches | 1,074 of 1,926 mixed low-band peaks and 1,273 kick-mic reference peaks. Median per-break proxy precision 57.5% and recall 95.0%. |
| Absolute timing error of matched peaks | Median 6.0 ms and p90 39.9 ms. The matching window bounds these results. |
| Local waveform correlation | 359 of 422 selected kicks had an absolute fit of 0.6 or higher, with both correlation signs occurring. |
| Snare-window low-band co-activation | 982 eligible windows. Median of per-break contamination proxies −49.3 dB, observed per-break medians −58.6 to −29.0 dB. |

These data support the full mixed performance as the playback reference and show why raw files cannot be overlaid or used as zero-offset ground truth. The built-in mixed-signal detector should stay conservative about only gently ducking bass. The experiment uses a differently specified peak finder, so these are proxy comparison results rather than measured precision for the shipped detector. For a prepared onset map, hand-label misleading fills and weak kick events, check manual labels against close-mic correlation and mixed transients, and preserve offsets separately from the audio timeline. Separate mic blending needs a broader phase and listening comparison.

### Pitch-preserving tempo pilot

A local Rubber Band 4.0.0 R3 offline comparison used three complete mixed breaks at 0.90× and 1.10× tempo. Each source output retained the full play order. The reference used stereo-linked SciPy rational polyphase varispeed. For both outputs, an identical 5 ms transient novelty detector matched output peaks to source peak positions mapped by the nominal tempo ratio. The one-to-one matching window was ±65 ms with short edge guards.

The R3 renders had exact expected length in all six cases, with 1,033 of 1,081 eligible peaks matched. Median and p90 absolute deviations were 2.04 and 14.74 ms. The polyphase reference was within one sample of expected length, with 1,060 of 1,082 peaks matched and deviations of 1.59 and 5.82 ms. R3 crest changes relative to varispeed ranged from −0.72 to +0.33 dB. Stereo channel correlation fell by 0.025–0.043 in these cases. This is a transient and stereo comparison on the given settings. The product uses its own shared-clock windowed-sinc converter with an exact output count. Keeping the product's varispeed leaves less measured timing displacement in this experiment. A future pitch-preserving option needs an equal-loudness audition and embedded-library license review. [Rubber Band offline API and engines](https://breakfastquay.com/rubberband/code-doc/), [Rubber Band licensing](https://breakfastquay.com/rubberband/)
