# Ski Beatz

[← Producers](README.md)

**Who.** Producer of Jay-Z's "Dead Presidents", "Can I Live" and the Camp Lo and Curren$y records. Studied 2026-11-19 for the order of operations: choose attractive sounds first, fit tempo later.

## Signature technique (as the source describes it)

Ableton's written account of a filmed demonstration (26 January 2010): Ski listens with the metronome off, adds roughly 10 to 15 markers to desirable events without relating them to session tempo, slices at the markers, plays the resulting keyboard bank, taps a tempo with the other hand and records. The purpose is to discover a new performance quickly from selected sounds. It is Ableton's contemporaneous written account; the embedded video was not watched, and it is modern Ableton technique, not evidence that an MPC60 had Warp Markers.

## What Liminal took

- **Select before you fit.** Liminal already has a drummer and a tempo, so speech must not replace that authority. The transferable part is to find clear, characterful units first and ask which can fit the current beat. A sentence is not a periodic drum loop and may have no native BPM; fit one or several stress anchors, not an imaginary even syllable grid. A shorter good phrase can beat a perfectly bar-sized bad one ([handoff](../dialogue-chopping/handoff.md), "Ski Beatz").
- It challenges a universal "stretch the whole source, then chop" recipe. This is a design principle from the handoff; the implementation record is the [plan](../../plans/mpc-chopping.md) and decisions [D124](../../decisions/music.md#d124-dialogue-lines-are-chosen-by-how-interesting-their-words-are-2026-11-21) to [D127](../../decisions/music.md#d127-the-chop-beat-mixes-its-pieces-plays-off-the-drums-and-has-a-feel-per-loop-2026-11-2122).
