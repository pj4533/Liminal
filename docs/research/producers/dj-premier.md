# DJ Premier

[← Producers](README.md)

**Who.** Producer of Gang Starr and much New York boom bap. Studied for his small, deliberately performed sample vocabulary: one short stab, a pitched sibling of it on another pad, alternated. Studied 2026-11-19 for the dialogue chopping engine.

## Signature techniques (as the sources describe them)

- **Restart the stab, pitch a copy down, flip-flop pads** (confirmed, with detail). In the Red Bull Music Academy lecture (Toronto 2007) he says the source for "The Question Remains" was a Bob James record damaged by a paper stuck to it, that he "kept chopping it and kept starting it over right at that one little stab", then "pitched it down one time and Guru was like, 'Yo, let's just leave it like that'" and "put it on a different pad and then flip-flopped from then." He also says "I started doing stab-type samples just to strip down more" and defines stabs as "a short dash." The MPC60 is not mentioned in that lecture text, so the anecdote must not be tied to a specific machine.
- **Selection is situated.** The Wax Poetics interview has him learning loops on an S900, moving to an MPC60 for programming after Eddie Sancho introduced it, and making beats in the room from a fresh start, responding to the artist. This is first-person history, not a technical specification.
- **Pitch and tempo figures** (secondary, lower weight). A ModeAudio article, in the author's own words and drawn from WhoSampled, says "Mass Appeal" uses a roughly 2 second Vic Juris section slowed from 118 to 96 BPM and pitched down about 4 semitones, and that the "N.Y. State of Mind" drums are a nearly unedited break slowed about 16 BPM from about 100. These are not Premier testimony.
- **Bass.** Cited with Pete Rock and Just Blaze for low-passing a sample loop to keep its feel, or filtering the lows out and writing a new line locked to the drums, and for pitch-down in the sampler lowering pitch and tempo together (gritty, dark). See [boom bap bass](../bass-and-pads/boombap-bass.md).
- **Unverified.** His scratch-style hooks are described as collages of cut vocal samples, and he described cutting the "Rite Where You Stand" hook, but no source said which cuts or how they were performed. Scratch behaviour is not documented here and anything built to imitate it is an invention.

## What Liminal took

- **A small related vocabulary carries identity** ([handoff](../dialogue-chopping/handoff.md), "DJ Premier"). One anchor piece, a few siblings and a few stabs, repeated exactly before anything changes. This became the hook's roles in [D125](../../decisions/music.md#d125-the-motif-becomes-a-hook-one-2-bar-cell-on-fixed-steps-varied-only-at-the-phrase-ends-2026-11-21): a signature piece S, a response R and a third piece T on fixed steps of a 2-bar cell.
- **Stabs as short dashes.** Short gated hits of the same pad, in [D126](../../decisions/music.md#d126-tight-gated-dialogue-chops-waveform-onsets-per-hit-gates-and-a-step-grid-that-follows-only-its-own-drum-hit-2026-11-21) and [D127](../../decisions/music.md#d127-the-chop-beat-mixes-its-pieces-plays-off-the-drums-and-has-a-feel-per-loop-2026-11-2122).
- **Playback rate.** D125 kept the original rate and left Premier's alternating pitched pads in the research notes. D130 added one linked speed-and-pitch adjustment per line, limited to ±2.5 semitones, to fit the speech cadence to the beat. [Craft §1.1](../dialogue-chopping/craft.md#11-dj-premier-the-question-remains-confirmed-with-detail) · [D130](../../decisions/music.md#d130-continuation-salience-learned-footage-and-chop-feel-2026-11-23)
- A "Premier-inspired operation" here means related sample variants and repeated stabs. It does not mean a Premier sound preset or a copy of a record.
