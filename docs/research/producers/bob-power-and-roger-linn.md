# Bob Power and Roger Linn

[← Producers](README.md)

**Who.** Bob Power is the engineer and producer behind A Tribe Called Quest, De La Soul and D'Angelo records. Roger Linn designed the LinnDrum and the swing and pad timing of the Akai MPC. Neither is a beatmaker in the sense of the rest of this folder; they are the evidence on timing and the sampler's feel. Studied 2026-11-19.

## What the sources say

- **Power** explains that a sampled part carries the timing of its original musical context, and that removing that friction on replay can remove what made it interesting.
- **Linn** describes delaying even sixteenths (systematic swing), velocity-sensitive performance, pressure-controlled repeated notes, accurate playback and tightly trimmed samples. He rejects a secret random timing engine as the explanation of the MPC feel. His design account is primary for his machines, not a claim about every Dilla decision.

## What Liminal took

- **Timing needs intention and clean boundaries.** Preserve a sample's internal phrasing while choosing particular relationships to the current drummer. Not every mismatch is desirable. Treat a sample's linguistic entrance and its rhythmic accent separately (a consonant can precede the stressed vowel that should coincide with a drum, so schedule the lead-in before the accent), and keep a repeated gesture's contour ([handoff](../dialogue-chopping/handoff.md), "Bob Power and Roger Linn").
- **No "humanize with random offsets" shortcut.** A single stable offset per gesture, within about 25 ms: [D127](../../decisions/music.md#d127-the-chop-beat-mixes-its-pieces-plays-off-the-drums-and-has-a-feel-per-loop-2026-11-2122) implements fixed per-position feel offsets, and the code comment likens the push or drag to an MPC's swing. See also [J Dilla](j-dilla.md).
