# Kaytranada

[← Producers](README.md)

**Who.** Producer of *99.9%* and *BUBBA*, whose influence Rolling Stone ties to Dilla. Studied 2026-11-19 for placement of chops on off-beat sixteenths. This is third-party analysis of a house and R&B-adjacent producer; no first-hand vocal-chop method was found.

## What the sources say

A LiveSchool analysis of one track describes chops mostly on off-beat sixteenths (the second, third or fourth sixteenth of the beat), swung chops, a repeated chop that stays the same until a breakdown, and a pitch change on every fourth repeat. A Sample Focus guide describes deliberately unquantized notes for a laid-back feel.

## What Liminal took

The placement bias, in [craft §2.3](../dialogue-chopping/craft.md#23-kaytranada-style-placement-new-analysis-level): stressed-syllable landmarks toward the "e" and "a" of the beat, with the clearest word well away from a kick, and one parameter changed on every fourth repeat. The chop beat of [D126](../../decisions/music.md#d126-tight-gated-dialogue-chops-waveform-onsets-per-hit-gates-and-a-step-grid-that-follows-only-its-own-drum-hit-2026-11-21) and [D127](../../decisions/music.md#d127-the-chop-beat-mixes-its-pieces-plays-off-the-drums-and-has-a-feel-per-loop-2026-11-2122) places stabs in gaps, answering the snare, in ghost-note pockets and as a pickup on the "a" into a downbeat. Those decisions do not cite him.
