# Pete Rock

[← Producers](README.md)

**Who.** Producer (Pete Rock and CL Smooth, "T.R.O.Y.", "The Joy"). Studied for his view that playback speed is a choice about character, and for longer, near-intact phrases. Studied 2026-11-19.

## Signature techniques (as the sources describe them)

- **Native speed on purpose.** In the NPR Microphone Check interview (2014) he says he wanted "to sample the actual singing on 33, the way it was made" for "The Joy" and that "certain things only sound right on 45." This is a source-specific judgement, not a rule to always or never repitch. He identifies the beat as an older SP-1200 beat from 1997/98, redone on the MPC with the vocal added.
- **Longer loops.** He says he "stopped sampling half a bar loops to one bar loops to making it bigger." That counters a "tiny chop only" bias: long, near-intact phrases are a legitimate producer choice.
- **Bass.** Cited with Premier and Just Blaze for low-passing a sample loop to isolate its bass or filtering the lows out and writing a new line locked to the drums. See [boom bap bass](../bass-and-pads/boombap-bass.md). No first-hand method detail on his guitar use was found.

## What Liminal took

- **Keep native cadence a real candidate.** Compare it with a linked-rate version when a shorter or darker gesture fits, and keep the original when speech character matters more ([handoff](../dialogue-chopping/handoff.md), "Pete Rock"). The same section corrects terminology: rate conversion couples duration and pitch, pitch-preserving time stretch is a separate operation, and pitch shifting alone a third.
- **Long phrases stay first-class** (1 to 2 bars, 2 to 6 s), per [craft §1.3](../dialogue-chopping/craft.md#13-pete-rock-the-joy-confirmed-nuanced).
- **The shipped chops play at native rate only** (user direction, [plan](../../plans/mpc-chopping.md); see [D125](../../decisions/music.md#d125-the-motif-becomes-a-hook-one-2-bar-cell-on-fixed-steps-varied-only-at-the-phrase-ends-2026-11-21), which declines the pitched sibling).
