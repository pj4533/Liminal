# Todd Edwards

[← Producers](README.md)

**Who.** House and UK garage producer known for vocal collage. Studied 2026-11-19 as a cross-genre comparison, not as a boom-bap exemplar. The bank-preparation decisions transfer.

## What the sources say

- **Prepared banks.** He describes laying drums down, finding a sound, trying it, rejecting it if it fails, and later preparing banks to experiment with.
- **Memory limits shaped sound.** He recalls sampling 33 RPM records at 45 to conserve memory, then playing them lower, producing artifacts; early sampling time was limited to roughly 5 to 15 seconds, and excessive pitch movement costs quality.
- **Spacious tracks despite larger capacity.** In the 5 Magazine interview he stresses preparation effort and a renewed appetite for older, spacious tracks. Quality depends on choosing and preparing usable ingredients, and available memory does not require density.

## What Liminal took

- **Prepare a stable bank, then play it.** The source-specific pad bank with stable roles, rather than rebuilding vocabulary every bar ([handoff](../dialogue-chopping/handoff.md), "Todd Edwards"; the hook roles of [D125](../../decisions/music.md#d125-the-motif-becomes-a-hook-one-2-bar-cell-on-fixed-steps-varied-only-at-the-phrase-ends-2026-11-21)).
- **Separate readable speech from vocal texture.** Most featured dialogue should convey recognisable language. Occasional tiny slices can act as percussion or atmosphere, with a distinct role and a strict density budget. A modern MPC Slice Motion tutorial (restricted random stutter) is sound design, not evidence that random syllables form sentences.
