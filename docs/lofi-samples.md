Every source is released under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), so credit is a courtesy rather than a condition. They were trimmed, resampled to 32 kHz, and level matched. Filenames identify the MIDI root note and, when present, its velocity layer.

The recorded Steinway uses seven root positions and velocity layers 2 and 3. The optional fourth filename field identifies an alternate layer. Tone shaping and stereo effects occur at render time.

Recorded octave and playback register are calibrated independently. FFT and period checks found that the upright pianos and pizzicato strings sound an octave above their source labels. The sampler compensates on playback while using the filename roots to preserve the intended warm register and spacing. The Steinway and concert harp already match their labeled roots. The 2026-11-19 additions below use measured concert-pitch MIDI roots in their bundled filenames.

## Additions on 2026-11-19 (D114)

The user asked for more acoustic, softer melody instruments that tuck into the mix, more kinds of piano with different amounts of effects, and guitar phrases. The vibraphone, marimba, quiet pipe organ, TX81Z FM piano, bass recorder and plucked psaltery were removed with their recordings. The table lists each new sample group, the programs that play it and the recordings it uses. Each number links the bundled MIDI root to its original recording, except for the two FreePats archives, which have no per-file links.

`scripts/lofi-sample-recipes.sh` rebuilds each group from its downloaded source with `scripts/prepare-lofi-samples.py`. The preparation decodes with FFmpeg, folds to mono and resamples to 32 kHz. It trims the leading silence 2 ms before the attack, applies the listed low-pass (second-order 7.5 kHz by default) and a 45 Hz high-pass, and matches the strongest 75 ms body to −10 dBFS under a −2.5 dBFS peak ceiling. Notes are kept to 2.4 s with a 0.6 s raised-cosine tail. The script measures each fundamental by subharmonic summation and refuses a root more than 50 cents from its filename. All bundled roots measured within 26 cents. The VCSL Kawai, VCSL Folk Harp and Osiris recordings sound an octave above their source labels, so the file labelled C2 is bundled as MIDI 48. `prepare-lofi-samples.py measure <wav>...` reports body level, peak, 2–5 kHz share and pitch for any bundled file.

## Bass body samples

The sampled bass body (the synthesized sub owns everything below 160 Hz, see `docs/plans/bass-tone-redo.md` 2 and D119) uses two CC0 kits. The `round` tone (one of the three that rotate every 16 bars, D120) plays the flatwound `long` files (the `ghost` files for ghosts) as its body and the first 80 ms of the flatwound `short` files as its strike; the `upright` tone plays the upright `long` files from their onset (an upright ghost is the long note at ghost velocity) with a synthesized strike; the `synth` tone uses no files. The upright `short` files are no longer played (the frozen throw send still reads them on upright takes). They are bundled as `LofiBass-<kit>-<stroke>-<midi>[-<rr>].wav`, which `LofiSampleBank` ignores because it reads only the `LofiLead` prefix. `scripts/lofi-sample-recipes.sh bass-upright` and `bass-flatwound` rebuild them. Both libraries sound an octave below their file labels (Sneakybass `e2` is E1, Swagbass `a1` is A0), so the bundled MIDI roots are 28 to 39 and 21 to 33. Preparation uses `--prefix LofiBass --retune`: 32 kHz mono, 25 Hz high-pass, 5 kHz low-pass, body matched to -10 dBFS under a -2.5 dBFS peak ceiling, and each file resampled by its measured pitch error. All 35 bundled files measure within 2.2 cents of their root.
