## Architecture

```
Audio                               Visual
-----                               ------
GenerativeEngine (facade)           CuratedImageFeed -> CuratedImagePool (disk) -> ImageQueue
 ArchiveConductor (own queue)        -> 3:2 centre crop (ImageFraming)
 LofiPlayback: drum transport,      EffectsUniformsComputer (single source of truth)
 phrase queue, clock                  -> EffectsMetalView (macOS, MTKView)
 LofiMasterBus (finisher)             -> OffscreenEffectsRenderer (visionOS, mono 2048 ->
        |                                DrawableQueue -> RealityKit shell)
        |  MusicState (lock-free)
        +-----> MusicReactor / MusicModulation -> uniforms
```

Images (`decisions/images.md` D36-D45, D48): `ImageQueue` runs one loop over `CuratedImageFeed` (`Liminal/Visual/ImageSources/`), which has two halves. Ingest runs on a background task, one picture at a time: a weighted rotation over the sources in `image-collections.json`, the provider's search or pinned ids, the rights and size gates, an https download, a bounded ImageIO decode, border trim, a 3:2 window for odd aspects, the openness, document, text and face gates, a duplicate check (catalogue aliases before download, a perceptual fingerprint after decode), colour analysis and a JPEG in `CuratedImageStore/` with its credit and openness in the sidecar. Display picks the next stored picture (never-shown pictures first, so nothing repeats while new ones wait; least recently shown when offline; source alternation, openness weighting, a monochrome cap) and reads only from disk, so the Lo-Fi reserve, offline use and everything after the queue (buffer, crop, saliency, depth, footage) work from the stored picture alone. The whole pipeline, every source and provider, and how to add one: [image-sources/](image-sources/README.md).

Shader pipeline order (`Liminal/Shaders/EffectsRenderer.metal`): Ken Burns, distortion (turbulence, log-spherical, sinusoidal), stereo parallax and chromatic aberration, crossfade, hue shift (spatial, saliency, fractal), contrast/saturation, ghost taps, stochastic bloom, feedback warping, vibrance. `EffectsUniforms.swift` must match the Metal struct exactly. Images use one orientation convention: texture row 0 is the top. Platform details: `PLATFORM_ARCHITECTURE.md`.

### Lo-Fi audio

The drum player queues one complete, stereo-linked 64-beat recording per take. At each take boundary it picks, by the session seed, one break among those within 4 BPM of the closest labeled tempo to the set BPM (never the take that just played), from the local `SlowBurnBeatGrid.json` catalog (Slow Burn Vol. 1–4, 100 breaks, D122), and resamples it to the set tempo. The bass, dry acoustic instruments, texture, and wet rack have independently scheduled buffers on the same sample grid. The five arranged stems start at one audio host time. Their first downbeat establishes the music clock and releases the visual and footage playback gate on macOS and visionOS. A sixth input carries dialogue and its effects onto the Lo-Fi bus at the scheduled hit times. Interior phrases are queued early so decoding the next drum take can overlap playback. If the next phrase still misses its deadline, playback repeats the preceding bass, dry instruments and texture on the same grid, carries the outgoing effect tail once, and reuses the current drum recording if a new take was due.

`LofiScore` builds 32-bar forms with remembered four-bar motifs and their own cadences. An acoustic piano anchors each 16-bar take. Three independently seeded rotations (`LofiPreset`: ten piano, four plucked string and seven guitar programs, several sharing a sample group with different tone, wow and space) choose a piano, a plucked string and a guitar. The two answering instruments take the middle phrases before the opening piano returns. Guitar programs add `.ornament` notes from `LofiGuitarPhrasing` around the unchanged melody: staggered strums (up to four strings on opening and landing notes, voiced with sevenths by `LofiStrumVoicing`), fingerpicked broken chords, double stops and grace notes. They render with the lead, and the mid shells keep clear of their onsets. A `.strum` note (`LofiStrum`, D128) renders into its own accent buffer beside the melody: it gets the lead's filters, gain curve and phrase gain but never enters the gain measurement, the compressor reference, the mids' level match and pad duck, or the phrase's instrument balance, its send is `0.032 + 0.33 * send` (0.065 for a plain stab, 0.33 for a thrown one; lead and ornament notes `0.44 + 0.72 * send`), and its `damp` chokes it after the hold. `LofiStrumHook` scores those notes last, after everything else in the phrase, for guitar and harp programs in a take's middle phrases: two to five choked stabs, dyads, holds or harp rolls a phrase (a call-and-answer statement answers with another voicing of the chord), repeated exactly at one sixteenth chosen against the drums (`LofiStrumDrums`: the take's kicks, full snares and ghost notes on the drummer's own swung grid, or a plain boom-bap bar without a timing field), never on a snare, a downbeat or the last sixteenth, a quarter beat clear of every melody and shell onset, nothing in a bar with three melody notes, and nothing in the phrase's last bar. The breakdown (take bars 9-12, where the chops hold single words) answers the snare and pushes into kicks; take bars 5-8 (the chop beat) play only guitars, 3 dB quieter, only with kicks or on an even gap, at most twice, in 60 percent of those phrases; harps roll only in the breakdown (70 percent of their phrases). The statement's last stroke is thrown into the echo when it starts by beat 10.5. Even takes play archetypes A-C and odd takes D-E, so consecutive takes never repeat one. The hook never changes the other parts, the ending rest or the record edit, and `LofiScore.Audition.hook` switches it off for captures. Each program carries a measured level trim applied with the phrase's dry lead gain. Dry attacks use a bounded gain based on the strongest 75 ms window. The renderer then measures the stereo sum of the instrument, texture and its effects, including the future tail. Peak, 400 ms and three-second limits set one downward gain for the phrase. Carried tails retain their previous gain, and the drums and bass bypass this instrument trim. `LofiRenderer` uses recorded instrument sources, separate note sends, and independent echo, room, and color returns. Grid-timed fragments use isolated acoustic notes for scale-aware pitch shifts, with reverse, flutter, spectral holds, and cross-modulation as alternate recipes. A short plate and a longer hall take both melody and midrange sends, including echoes and grains. It renders off the audio thread and keeps the audible wet tail at phrase joins. Kick candidates from the unchanged drum waveform provide sample-timed visual onsets and anchor nearby scored bass attacks to the drummer’s timing. The take's kick landmarks duck only the bass (see the bass chain below). A two-beat reverse pickup leads into the eight-bar breakdown. A four-beat octave-layered pickup leads into the next take. The renderer carries their releases across phrase joins. The live player publishes the take’s audible origin and measured beat duration. The footage scheduler preloads the next decoder and ends its dissolve on that same downbeat. The primary image uses the same audible downbeat. The queue reserves a distinct image ahead of time, with bundled art available when generation or cache decoding is late. Both renderers preload the texture. `LofiImageReveal` is a drop (`docs/plans/drop-transitions.md`, decisions T44–T50). `LofiFillDetector` finds where each take's halfway and turnaround fills start from the take's landmarks (published with `LofiTimingField.fills`); without drum takes the build is 2 or 4 beats. The `TransitionDirector`'s drop mode picks one of 20 glitch styles, a colour layer and a finisher. The outgoing picture breaks up from the fill start with an exponential build that jolts on the fill's hits, and both renderers evaluate it at the frame's presentation time (`displayLead`), so the first frame on or after the audible downbeat is an ordinary frame of the new picture: no transition uniforms, no feedback on that one frame, the new vibrance, the new relief, and the new picture's Ken Burns push starting there. The bell reveal (every change under `-LiminalDropOff`) keeps the surge, swap and settle family. Bloom, color and film strength crest on the downbeat and release at once.

The footage worker also analyzes up to 32 seconds of the incoming film audio. It estimates background power in three bands, detects rises in the speech envelope, and joins nearby syllables at quieter boundaries. The sampler alternates two-bar gestures. Short calls can repeat and answer at different speeds. Where a clip has a longer stretch of speech, the sampler looks for at least four onsets and a quiet ending, plays two fragments in order, then brings back the contiguous line on the next bar’s downbeat. The full line stays within 8% of its original speed. All three hits fit before the next group or drum fill. The closest phrase in the same cue can carry a pause or foreshadow a later line. A separate player schedules the finished samples against the drum transport with output latency compensation. The continuous film goes through the archive strip’s band-pass, phaser, dark echo and room. Its direct level stays low, with one brief clarity swell per picture and a soft dip under each drop. The on-device Sound Analysis classifier ranks spoken windows above song and choir; envelope cues remain available if classification produces no results. Each chop gets corrective presence EQ, bounded gain based on K-weighted 50 ms syllable levels, a moving window up to 400 ms and a separate peak guard. A short pickup can reverse. A dotted-eighth echo and a damped stereo room blend with the direct chop before its fader. The whole channel follows the footage control and cue fades. A repeat can divide at an interior syllable onset, pause, then finish on an eighth- or sixteenth-note subdivision. Clips without usable onsets keep the same background treatment.

The melody and midrange rack can pick up a short window from its own colored return and replay three grid-timed fragments, including reversed and octave-down answers. The fragments feed filtered echoes and room sends. A soft low-pass on the upper wet band and a linked peak control keep the return focused on the mids. The renderer carries at least nine seconds or eight beats of available wet tail into the next take at the original pitch and echo spacing.

The archive store protects ten curated titles and reserves two slots for discoveries within a shared 3 GB limit. At launch, off the main actor, the store deletes files its index does not list, and stale `CFNetworkDownload_*.tmp` files and old `liminal-*` fixtures are removed from `tmp/`; indexed clips are never touched by this cleanup. When both groups are available, the weaver schedules a discovery after four curated excerpts. After its second play and outgoing fade, a discovery becomes eligible for replacement. [Film credits](experience.md#pinned-films-and-rotating-discoveries)

An incoming footage cue can decode while the current cue plays. Late PCM starts at the matching position in the active excerpt with a short fade. The Lo-Fi archive rack picks a three-tap echo pattern every four audible bars. Each pattern uses beat subdivisions for its main echo, darker stereo answer, and room reflection. The tap levels swell independently. Two delay banks let earlier echoes decay at their original timing across changes. Footage and music are summed independently before the final bus. The combined final path uses light compression, slowly adjusts gain within bounded limits, and protects sample peaks with a conservative output trim. ### Dialogue chopping pipeline

Lo-Fi turns the film's speech into a small bank of related cuts and plays it against the drum take. The code is in `Liminal/Audio/Lofi/Dialogue/`; every stage is a pure function on preparation threads, and nothing here runs on the render thread.

1. **Source.** `DialogueSource` holds the immutable native-rate PCM of the incoming cue, its film start time and rate, and the cue token. `DialogueCueCoordinator` and `DialogueDirector` decode and run the stages below for each cue while the current cue plays. Analysis covers up to 32 seconds (`DialogueTuning.analysisSeconds`).
2. **Analysis.** `DialogueAnalyzer` combines the SoundAnalysis speech windows with envelopes in three bands, nuclei, voicing, pauses and clipping. A cue led by music or singing gives no bank.
3. **Hierarchy.** `DialogueHierarchy` builds `SpeechUnit`s from utterance to phrase, word group, stab and texture. Units keep their parent and source neighbours, stress anchors, protected spans and tails.
4. **Pad bank.** `DialogueBankBuilder` ranks units by sound before rhythm and keeps 8 to 12 pads (`DialoguePadBank`) with roles anchor, head, answer, stab, sibling and texture. With a transcript, `DialogueReorderGuard` may arrange a bank in a safe template (repeat a clause, omit a parenthetical, move an adverbial, exchange clauses); without one the source order stands.
5. **Motif and performer.** `DialogueMotif` is the default. One line per 16-bar loop is cut into word and syllable pieces and placed so every bar has an onset, following the drummer's timing field. Each loop takes a fresh line. If the cue's film has none, it takes one from the memory's crate of the last four films (`motifCrate`), and it carries the previous line only when no film offers one (D111). Lines are never reused while unplayed ones remain: `DialogueLineLedger` keeps every line that has led a loop, per film in film seconds, in `Application Support/Liminal/DialogueLines.json` across sessions; the motif skips any line overlapping a played one by more than half and picks among the three best unplayed lines (seeded). Only when the cue's film and the crate have nothing unplayed does it reuse the line played longest ago (D123). Among unplayed lines, `DialogueLineInterest` ranks transcribed lines by their words (D124): names, vocatives, numbers and a lexicon of evocative stems (drugs, dreams, death, space, love, vivid nouns and imperatives) weigh most, function words nothing. The value is sound times fit times `0.35 + 0.65 interest`; lines without text count 0.45, so the acoustic order is unchanged. A new loop takes the own film's lines that are not plain first, then a striking transcribed line from the crate (first, when the cue has no transcript yet), then any line. Each fragment carries the weight of its strongest word. The loop's roles come from one hook (D125): the strongest piece (the signature), an answering piece and a third, on one of four templates that answer on the backbeat (the response on a snare step) and rotate every two loops. The loop is arranged as a 16-bar arc (D126 part 2, `DialogueArc`): the intro states the whole line on bar 1's downbeat (the line tries only steps 0, 4, 8 and 16) with a tag in bar 2; the chop beat plays a 2-bar groove cell twice in bars 3 to 6, the second time with a new last beat. The cell (`DialogueChopBeat.swift`, D127) is the template's skeleton (a skeleton hit on a non-downbeat kick moves an 8th off it) filled to six stabs a bar, five when the drummer plays on 11 or more of the bar's sixteenths. Fill steps are scored against the take's modal kick, full-snare and ghost-note steps (`DialogueMotifDrums`; a ghost is a snare at most 0.45 of the take's typical strength): gaps between hits and the 8th after a backbeat snare score highest, ghost-note pockets and one swung "a" pickup into a downbeat per cell are the only odd sixteenths (at most two a cell), kicks other than the downbeat score lowest, and the drummer's busiest beat and flams are left alone. A seeded spread of the scores moves the fill every loop while the skeleton holds for the template's two loops. Each stab then takes the cut its site prefers (`GrooveVocabulary`: the signature's head and tail halves, the response's halves, the third piece, the line's other words last) that differs from the hits on either side, so consecutive hits use different pieces; a line that may not be reordered plays the new pieces of each run of a bar up to a signature hit in source order (its earlier words lead into the signature), and a site no cut fits is dropped. The only same-piece repeats are marked `stutter` (the build's rolls and the turnaround's stutter). The loop's `DialogueFeel` (`DialogueArc.feel`, one of grid, laid, rush, drunk and ahead, never the same twice in a row) adds a fixed offset of at most 25 ms to the chop beat's and build's stabs on the "and", "e" and "a"; quarter steps stay on the drummer's grid; the build doubles that into 16ths in bar 8 (halving, a ladder of the line's first words or ping-pong of two heads); the breakdown holds a word or two a bar with echo throws and a reversed pickup into bar 13; the outro keeps the return's quiet piece in bar 13, restates the line in bars 14 and 15 (walked on the 8ths with the signature stuttered, or whole with a word repeated) and tags bar 16. Each section has seeded variants, and consecutive loops' arcs differ in at least two of their five section fields (and in the feel) (a carried loop in the groove and two more; a transcript re-cut keeps the arc). Unfixed hits move by 8ths only; `follow` items stutter on consecutive 16ths by choking the hit before. The roles never settle on a function word while the line has a real word (a re-cut drops such an anchor; a line whose other pieces are all function words answers with its signature). A section's first piece may start up to `sectionPreRollSeconds` (0.28 s) before its downbeat when the cue window opens earlier (the previous plan's echo tails end `sectionTailLead`, 0.3 s, before the boundary), so bar 9's downbeat hit lands; no piece starts in the bed's first 0.2 s (`bedStartLeadSeconds`), because the voice queues a cue's chops only once its bed has begun. Hook items never slide off their step, and coverage repair (a bar's onset is a hit's accent) tries the hook's own item first and holds its word in the breakdown. Pads are tight and gated (D126). Every fragment starts at its waveform onset (`DialogueOnset`: a 2 ms RMS envelope of the 200 Hz to 4.2 kHz band, the first syllable peak within 6 dB of the loudest value of the first 300 ms, the sound walked back to the end of the last 15 ms below a tenth of that peak); its accent, the frame on the step, is that peak's half-way crossing, and a piece starts at most 30 ms (2 ms pre-roll, 2 or 5 ms fade) before it, the line at most 120 ms. A transcribed piece never starts inside a word: when its first syllable is more than 6 dB under the next, that syllable becomes the beat (`weakLead`, the first peak within 12 dB) instead of being trimmed. The arranged pads are then rendered once each (`DialogueMotif.renders`) and their accents re-read on the render, because the chop chain's 4:1 compressor brings a soft onset's rendered attack up to 20 ms earlier; every hit is a prefix of its pad's render (`DialogueChopRenderer.prefix`), so the render stage runs the chain once per pad. Each hook item has a gate in sixteenths (`DialogueMotifCell.Item.gate`; stutter repeats 1, a hit one or two steps before the next held to it, otherwise 1/8 stabs for the signature and third and a quarter for the response, the cell's last response and the line played out); `DialogueMotifPerformer.gatedEnd` cuts the hit 6 ms before the step its gate reaches on the drummer's grid, or at a syllable valley or word end up to 35 ms before that, and the renderer closes it with a raised-cosine release of at most 8 ms at the full pad's levelling gain. The step grid is `LofiTimingField.sixteenthOffsets`: each confident kick or snare belongs to one step at most, a step without its own hit takes its beat position's median offset, and offsets are clamped to ±0.06 beat on 8th positions and -0.04 to +0.19 beat on the swung sixteenths, so a step no longer borrows a neighbour's hit. The coordinator writes the ledger when a plan is committed; unit tests and automated DEBUG launches (any automation flag) get an in-memory ledger, so they never consume the user's played lines. When `DialogueMotif.plan` returns nil, `DialoguePerformer` runs the bounded grammar families (anchor, echo, call-response, sparse-reorder, texture-fill). Both produce a `DialoguePerformancePlan` of blocks and `DialogueEvent`s. The plan changes at least every 16 bars.
6. **Chop render.** `DialogueChopRenderer` renders each event once, then caches the buffer. Each render runs a linked-rate resample, noise weighting, the chop chain and the per-piece effects: echo throws, reversed pickups and filtered repeats from the motif cells.
7. **Scheduling.** `ArchiveVoice.perform` stores a `ChopPlan` and schedules its chops sample-accurately on the transport clock. The film bed keeps playing from its own path and is never held back by analysis. Chops are placed only at or after the commit margin and a gesture the build overran is dropped whole, so a late plan never forces an immediate entrance.
8. **Token and revision rules.** A plan carries `cueToken` and `revision` (and the transport revision it was built against). `ArchiveVoice` keeps the highest revision per token and ignores a stale token or a revision no higher than the stored one. Revision 0 is the acoustic plan. A higher revision (the transcript-refined plan) replaces stored chops from `replaceFrom` on. A route or latency change is handled through the transport revision: `LofiPlayback` bumps `transportRevision`, the director sees the new snapshot revision, keeps the analysis and bank, and replans from the commit margin on, replacing chops that are not yet queued. Queued audio is never moved. A route recovery (`GenerativeEngine.recoverStoppedGraph`) stops the conductor, and its restart resets the voice: the bed stops and the chop fader stays closed, so the voice retires the current cue's token like a cancelled cue (`archiveChopsCancel`). A plan for that cue that arrives afterwards, whether a stale one still in flight on the old transport revision or the rebuild on the new grid, stores and queues nothing and ducks nothing; the footage service's next cue has a new token.
9. **Cache.** `DialogueDirector` keeps 12 analyses and banks keyed by content id, start (to 10 ms), frame count, playback rate (to 0.001) and analysis version; an entry on another time base or length is a miss. Entries do not keep the decoded PCM. Each plan's timings and log line record `thermalState`, `residentMB`, `cacheEntries` and `cacheBytes`. Transcript-refined banks are cached per `locale|modelVersion`. Analysis is cached independently of the plan, so a tempo change remaps the plan without redoing analysis.

Transcription is optional (`DialogueTranscriptionMode`: off, installed only by default, or allow one model download). `AppleSpeechTranscriptProvider` uses Apple SpeechAnalyzer and SpeechTranscriber off the scheduling path. The acoustic bank is built first and used whenever transcription is off, unavailable or late. The director waits for the transcript before the first plan for at most 1.2 s from the cue's start (`transcriptFirstWait`), and never past 0.45 s plus the commit margin before the window, so a transcript that is ready in time chooses and cuts the loop's line from bar 1 (D124); the bed and the music never wait.

Handoff record names and their code types, from [the research handoff](research/dialogue-chopping/handoff.md):

| Handoff record | Code type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SourceAudio` | `DialogueSource` | `contentID`, `cueToken`, immutable PCM, film start and rate; the frame/second conversion lives here |
| `SpeechUnit` | `SpeechUnit` (levels in `SpeechUnit.Level`) | frames, parent and neighbour ids, text and token range, confidences, stress anchors, protected spans, tail |
| `PadBank` | `DialoguePadBank` of `DialoguePad` | ordered pads, anchor pad, sibling groups, rejections, reorder arrangement |
| `TransportSnapshot` | `DialogueTransportSnapshot` | revision, take index, origin, beat durations (current and next), phrase at origin, kick and snare landmarks, lead notes, groove offsets |
| `PerformanceEvent` | `DialogueEvent` | pad, slot, accent and start time, gain, `chokeAt`, transform, cell and effect |
| (performance plan) | `DialoguePerformancePlan`, `DialogueBlock` | cue token, bank id, transport revision, plan revision |

Handoff fields with no direct counterpart:

- `speakerIDIfReliable` is omitted on purpose. Speaker identity was not reliable enough on this material to rank or group by.
- `expiryPolicy` has no field. A plan expires through the cue token (a departed clip is discarded) and the revision rules above.
- `dryChokeGroup` is not a named group. The one-voice rule is enforced per event through `chokeAt`: a later event's start shortens the earlier one, which ends 2 ms before it.
- `committedNextTempo` is carried as `nextBeatDuration` only; the committed-next key has no field.
- `latestCommitFrame` and `futureBoundaryFrames` are not stored. The planner derives the boundaries from `origin`, `beatDuration` and the phrase index, and the earliest start is a call argument.

### Per-bus chains and the master finisher

Each section has its own chain, built offline on preparation threads by `LofiStemChains` (zero latency: minimum-phase biquads and feed-forward dynamics, so landmarks found on unprocessed audio still point at the same frame) and the chop chain in `DialogueChopRenderer`.

- **Drums**: mono below 100 Hz, high-pass 32 Hz, a 320 Hz cut, a 3:1 bus compressor, 25 percent parallel 8:1 compression, tape-style saturation that rounds peaks until the take's crest reaches the target, a gentle air shelf, and a -4 dB peak ceiling. Kick attacks stay on their original frames.
- **Bass** (D116, tone from D119): `LofiBassVoice` plays one of three tones per phrase (D120): `LofiBassTone.tone(seed:phrase:)` picks a seeded tone for each 16-bar drum break (`phrasesPerTone` = 4 phrases), never the same as the block before, so a seed replays the same order; the capture hook `bassTone` pins one tone instead (`round` is `standard`, the reference tone the tests use; `upright` and `synth` are the other two). Everything below 160 Hz is an additive, phase-exact sub (fundamental plus real 2nd to 4th partials, each weighted by a zero-phase 160 Hz crossover); everything above comes from one dark layer: the CC0 flatwound (`round`) or onset-trimmed upright sample, undriven, high-passed at the same 160 Hz with the complementary zero-phase slope, levelled to a steady 13 dB under the sub and low-passed at 24 dB per octave (400 Hz `round`, 360 Hz `upright`), or (`synth`) the sub's series continued to the 12th partial. A note with `strike` (kick-anchored notes: 1 on downbeat, kick and weight notes, 0.6 on other anchored notes) also gets a short 300-650 Hz strike layer (the flatwound pluck, or synthesized partials), brightest in its first milliseconds and exactly zero by 80 ms, which the renderer places on the kick's broadband attack (`LofiScore.bassStrikeShift`), a brief opening of the body's low-pass and a +2.9 dB level accent. The chain: high-pass 30 Hz, gentle parallel harmonics in a zero-phase 200-450 Hz band (drive 2, mix 0.15), a 4:1 compressor, EQ (+0.5 dB at 80 Hz, -1 dB at 300 Hz, low-pass 1.4 kHz at 24 dB per octave), output trim, then the kick duck split at 150 Hz (full depth below, a third above, keyed by the take's kick landmarks; kicks that a note starts on are not ducked). `LofiScore+Bass` writes the line as a two-bar riddim cell per take from the take's own confident kicks: every note on a kick is anchored so its 40-160 Hz rise lands on the kick's (`lowRiseLead`, cached per voice, stroke, note and strike); anchored notes and holds sustain to the next onset (the note before a struck note ends 30 ms before it with a 45 ms release, other joins cross-fade over 40 ms), the note before a pickup may keep a breath, a note never fades out under a kick it is not anchored to, and breakdowns drop out for the last beats. `LofiBassThrow` renders dub throws through the frozen D116 send (`LofiBassThrowSend`: the body alone through a 0.75-beat echo and a small spring) into the space arrays before the space chain, so they reach the wet stem and the low end stays dry and mono. Breakdown phrases may sweep the body's low-pass from 150 to 450 Hz (`LofiScore.bassSweep`). The renderer finishes the bass before the space chain. Two capture-only translation tools sit there, both off in the app (D129): clean cue partials of each note (`LofiBassVoice.Cue`, added to the chain after the parallel harmonics and the compressor) and a cue duck (`LofiCueDuck`: the finished bass's 180-450 Hz band keys a zero-phase reduction of the texture and of the return's output before the bass throws). The `tune.bassCueDb` and `tune.cueDuck` hooks turn them on.
- **Lead and chords**: the lead gets 2.8 kHz presence. The chord channel (D117) is a held pad plus at most one answer shell per phrase. `LofiPadVoice` freezes the phrase program's own sample into overlapping Hann grains (two independent streams for width, a 0.3 s swell, a 0.85 s release that ends inside the phrase, a slow tremolo), voiced third, seventh and fifth or ninth with no root, at MIDI 51 to 62, with one legato inner revoicing at bar 3. The pad chain is a 300 Hz high-pass, -3 dB at 380 Hz, -4.5 dB at 2 kHz and a 2.2 kHz low-pass, mono below 300 Hz (D118). The pad is matched 7 dB under the lead in 400 Hz to 3 kHz where both sound and ducks 2 dB under the dry lead; the shells keep their own gain, 4 dB under the lead, with a 180 Hz high-pass and a 400 Hz cut. `LofiRenderer+Texture` keeps the pad and shell gains apart, and the pad has its own input to the reverb (plate and room mostly, little echo). The shells are mono below 300 Hz.
- **Space** (reverb and delay returns): high-pass 350 Hz, low-pass 4.5 kHz, ducked by the dry lead (5 dB) so notes are heard dry first: full depth on note onsets, a quarter of it while notes sustain, open in the lead's rests.
- **Dialogue chops**: the chop chain glues each cut to a consistent level and tucks it into the music, with the high-pass at 200 Hz, cuts at 300 Hz and 2.8 kHz, a narrow dip at 650 Hz that leaves the lead's body clear, and a 4.2 kHz low-pass. `ArchiveVoice.ChopBus` returns heavy echo and a long, dark room, and the music ducks only about 1 dB under featured chops (D112). The film bed sits under the chops and the music (D107).
- **Film bed**: the effected soundtrack path in `ArchiveVoice` and the footage mix bus, kept dark, with a one-bar dip under each theme return. The return downbeat comes from the transport (the cue's `LofiTransportInfo`: the fourth phrase of each 16-bar take, per `LofiScore.section(at:)`) and, as a live estimate, from the conductor's phrase callback (`ArchiveVoice.notePhrase`). Plan events are a fallback for captures without a transport, so a film with no speech and no chops still dips.

`LofiMasterBus` takes both branches (the Lo-Fi bus and the archive mix) in at -2 dB (`lofiStagingDB`), so the pre-master sum keeps headroom, and the finisher's input gain (+4 dB) makes those 2 dB up and adds 2 dB of drive (D121). `LofiMasterBus` hosts `LofiMasterKernel`, an audio unit whose render path allocates nothing: input trim, 25 Hz high-pass, a 1.7:1 glue compressor with a 100 Hz sidechain high-pass, a 4x oversampled soft clip from -2 dBFS, and a 3 ms look-ahead true-peak limiter at -1 dBTP. The reference capture reads about -13.9 LUFS, and live calibration steers toward -14 LUFS (`lofiTargetLUFS`) by at most +2 / -3 dB, holding upward steps while the limiter works hard or the soft clip passes 1 dB in an epoch. The kernel exposes meters and a gain-reduction trace for the capture harness (`LofiCaptureHooks`). Targets and measured results are in [mix verification](mix-verification.md) and [the measurements](plans/mpc-chopping-measurements.md).

**Recording** ([recording.md](recording.md)). After `LofiMasterBus` the graph ends in `GenerativeEngine.recordTap`, a unity mixer whose only job is to carry the recorder's tap (the master's own tap is the Lo-Fi calibration's). `FrameRecorder` copies each rendered frame into an IOSurface pixel buffer inside that frame's command buffer (macOS `EffectsMetalView.draw`, visionOS `OffscreenEffectsRenderer.renderAndPresent`), and appends it to an HEVC track on its own queue; the mix goes through a lock-free ring, with one time stamp per tap buffer, into 24-bit LPCM. Both tracks share one movie clock that starts at the first frame's presentation (`RecordingTiming.swift`): each 60 Hz slot holds the frame presented nearest its time at any render rate, and each audio buffer is placed where it is heard, with silence for anything lost (D47). `RecordingController` saves the movie to Photos.

- [AudioKit](https://github.com/AudioKit/AudioKit), AudioKitEX and SoundpipeAudioKit: audio synthesis and effects
- [ShaderGraphCoder](https://github.com/praeclarum/ShaderGraphCoder): RealityKit shader materials
- Apple frameworks: SwiftUI, Metal, RealityKit, ARKit, Core ML, Sound Analysis and Vision
